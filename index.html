About

I am an agricultural economist who uses behavioral and experimental economics to study how farmers and consumers perceive and respond to risk in the food system, and how information and market design can improve their decisions.

Much of my work centers on water. Scarcity is a major production risk for farmers, and non-conventional sources such as recycled water and stormwater can reduce that risk while raising food safety concerns for consumers. I study how farmers' beliefs about future scarcity shape their willingness to adopt these sources, why small and medium farms underinvest in irrigation, and how water market design affects efficiency and equity. On the consumer side, I study stigma toward produce grown with non-conventional water and how the content and messenger of information change perceived food risk.

I combine laboratory and survey experiments, discrete choice experiments, and belief elicitation with field data from farmers, consumers, and coastal communities. I am an Assistant Professor at Clemson University and Water Economist at the South Carolina Water Resources Center.
